# How Raven's scoring works

Short version: the online versions do not simply count your right answers. They estimate your ability from which items you got right and how hard those items were, then report it as a percentile against a comparison group the employer picks. There is no published pass mark.

## Online versions: ability scores, not raw scores

Both online versions use what TalentLens calls a **theta score**, an ability estimate from item response theory.

- **APM-III.** TalentLens's FAQ says each answer is scored on three things: whether it is correct, how difficult the item is, and how well the item separates stronger from weaker candidates. Getting the hardest, most discriminating items right earns a higher score than getting only easy ones right. Theta scores run from minus 4 to plus 4. [Source](https://www.talentlens.com/content/dam/school/global/Global-Talentlens/fr/documents-solutions/DOCUMENTS-RAVEN/TALENTLENS_TLO_APM_III_FAQ_v022019.pdf)
- **Raven's Adaptive.** Each item is coded by difficulty. You start on an item of average difficulty; a right answer brings a harder item, a wrong answer an easier one. TalentLens says someone who answers 15 difficult questions gets a different theta score from someone who answers 15 easy ones. [Source](https://www.talentlens.com/raven-adaptive.html)

What this means for you: on both versions, **the items you get right on harder questions count for more**. On the adaptive version you cannot see your score from how many you think you got right, because a candidate who is doing well sees harder items and may feel less sure.

## Percentile and norm groups

Theta scores are hard to read, so the reports turn them into **percentiles**: the share of a comparison group (a norm group) that you scored the same as or above. TalentLens's APM-III FAQ gives this example: a theta of minus 1.5, where 80% of the reference group score above that, is the 20th percentile. The highest percentile reported is the 99th.

Employers choose the comparison group. The APM-III FAQ uses managers as its example, since you would compare a manager candidate with other managers. The same performance can therefore produce a different percentile in different hiring processes.

## Paper versions: raw score and percentile

The classic paper APM produces a single raw score (the number correct) and a percentile rank against a norm group, according to [Pearson's product page](https://www.pearsonassessments.com/store/en/usd/p/100000414.html). Raven's 2 adds a standard score.

## What your report shows

For Raven's Adaptive, TalentLens says the profile report gives your percentile rank and several other norm-referenced scores. Theta scores themselves are not shown, because percentiles are easier to interpret and compare.

## What counts as a good score

There is **no published pass mark**. Each employer sets its own threshold and chooses its own comparison group, and Pearson does not publish employer cut-offs. If your invitation or the employer's careers page says how the result is used, that is the only reliable guide.

What you can control is your accuracy on the item types you will see. A practical aim is to get progression, rotation and distribution items right every time, because they carry the same weight in your accuracy as harder items and they are where careless losses happen.

## Guessing and blanks

Pearson does not publish a guessing penalty for the online versions. On APM-III, an unanswered item cannot be right, so if time is about to run out, choose the option that survives the most of your checks rather than leaving it blank.

## Unsupervised tests and re-testing

The APM-III FAQ advises employers to treat unsupervised results with caution and to confirm them later, for example with a supervised re-test or another exercise that measures the same skill. So expect that a strong online score may be checked again later in the process. Preparing properly, rather than relying on outside help, protects you at that stage.

Retake rules are not published by Pearson. They depend on the employer.
